An Ecology Field Practical

On a pair of sunny mornings, students congregate in a clump of woodland. They’re armed with measuring tape, long pieces of bamboo, and various other doodads of scientific origin, here to put theory into practice. Practicals might seem rote, but they’re an important aspect of education, where students gain an understanding of real-world conditions and develop their skills in an exposed but supervised setting. They learn by doing, which for some is additionally more useful than simply reading or observing.

In this particular practical, the students of the Laboratory of Ecology and Conservation study the structure and composition of vegetation: how to collect data, to monitor the woodland, and eventually how to determine changes in its composition, which can be an indicator of climate change, among other influences. They use several sampling methods to achieve this, and each one has its own conceptual underpinnings and usefulness. The idea is not just to learn each method but executing them out with the consistency and precision that enables replication. As noted by our narrator, Fina, this can be surprisingly difficult to do, but it’s ever so satisfying when the students finally figure it out.
